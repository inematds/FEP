Module 1: AI and LLM Fundamentals

Foundation

  • What it is: Large Language Models are neural networks trained on billions of texts to understand and generate language.

    Why: Understanding how the model "thinks" lets you create effective prompts.

    Concepts: Token prediction, attention, temperature, hallucinations.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Tokens are pieces of text that the model processes. The context window is the total limit.

    Why: Knowing how to count tokens helps you optimize prompts and avoid truncation.

    Concepts: ~4 characters = 1 token, context windows from 4K to 200K+, cost per token.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Ideal structure: context/role, instructions, input data, output format.

    Why: Well-structured prompts produce more consistent responses.

    Concepts: System vs. user, delimiters, examples, constraints.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Be specific and direct, eliminating ambiguity.

    Why: The clearer your request, the better the response.

    Concepts: Avoid vague pronouns, specify the format, define the length.

Module 2: Core Techniques

Technique

  • What it is: Ask the model to perform a task without providing examples—just clear instructions.

    Why: The simplest and fastest technique. Works for familiar tasks.

    Concepts: Direct instructions on when to use and when to avoid.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Provide 2-5 examples before asking for the response. The model learns the pattern.

    Why: Dramatically improves quality for specific tasks.

    Concepts: Representative examples, consistency, ideal quantity (3–5).

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Ask the model to "think aloud," showing each step of its reasoning.

    Why: Dramatically improves responses in logic, math, and decision-making.

    Concepts: "Think step by step," reasoning validation.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Give the model a role: "You are an expert in...".

    Why: Automatically adjusts tone, vocabulary, and technical depth.

    Concepts: Defining expertise, tone of voice, and role limitations.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

  • What it is: Techniques for making text sound human-written.

    Why: Increases authenticity and engagement. Avoids an “AI tone.”

    Concepts: Contractions, personal opinions, structural variation.

    View Full ⬇️ MD

Structure
